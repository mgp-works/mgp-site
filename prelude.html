Prelude

Project overview

Prelude is a mobile app that analyzes a photo of a meal using AI and cross-references it against trusted health sources to flag ingredients pregnant users should be cautious about, based on their individual health profile. For the app's first post-launch update, I led a redesign of the result screen — the core touchpoint where users see whether their meal is safe — to help users understand the outcome faster and trust it more.

Result screen mockup ("안전 보고서" / safety report UI) in phone frame — source PDF p.2

Context and problem

The original result screen presented ingredient-level safety information as a flat list of nutrients alongside raw source URLs. Users had no fast way to tell whether a meal was overall safe, and the sourcing looked unverified, which undermined trust in the recommendation.

AS-IS result screen — flat nutrient list + raw URLs — source PDF p.4 (AS-IS)

My role and responsibilities

I was the sole designer on a two-person team (with one iOS developer), owning UX research, information architecture, UX writing, and UI design for the redesign. I contributed 50% to planning and 100% to design.

Research and key insights

To validate the redesign direction, I ran an 11-day online survey with 26 pregnant users (61% in their first trimester, 84% first-time pregnancies), recruited through pregnancy and parenting communities. Three findings shaped the redesign:

  • Users default to avoiding any food with even a small chance of risk (50% picked the most risk-averse response option), so the screen needed to surface the safety verdict before any detail.
  • Users cared more about how a food affects their and their baby's health than about raw nutrient values (88.5% wanted to know health impact rather than nutrient content).
  • Sourcing felt unreliable: 76.9% said available information wasn't detailed enough, and 53.8% found conflicting information across sources confusing.

These findings set two redesign objectives: restructure information so the safety verdict is immediately clear, and change how sources are presented so users trust the answer.

Survey response charts (risk-avoidance %, info-source confusion %) — source PDF p.3

Design process

Information structure

I reordered the result screen so a danger/caution/safe summary sits at the top, with detailed ingredient information below sorted danger → caution → safe — matching how users scan for the verdict first.

AS-IS vs. TO-BE result screen comparison (summary block added) — source PDF p.4–5

Source presentation

I replaced bare source URLs with the issuing institution's name (e.g., "World Health Organization (who.int)") linked to the original page, so users can judge credibility at a glance instead of parsing a URL.

AS-IS URL list vs. TO-BE institution-name citation — source PDF p.5

Content tone

Since users cared about health impact over nutrient counts, I rewrote result copy to lead with the effect on maternal/fetal health rather than listing values. I also put together a UX writing guideline (documented in Notion, with AI assistance in compiling writing examples and reviewing tone) to keep language calm and reassuring — stating necessary cautions clearly without alarmist phrasing, and pairing warnings with a concrete alternative where possible.

AS-IS vs. TO-BE writing comparison (nutrient list → health-impact copy) — source PDF p.6

Final solution

The shipped result screen leads with a summary of danger/caution/safe counts, followed by ingredient-level detail ordered by risk, source citations shown as named institutions, and copy that explains health impact in a reassuring tone. Working with the iOS developer, I also helped scope a food-selection step: instead of the AI committing to a single (sometimes incorrect) food match after a long analysis, the app now shows the top 3 visual matches for the user to confirm before running the full analysis. The underlying analysis method and prompts were optimized during development.

Old flow (single-shot recognition) vs. new flow (top-3 selection step) diagram — source PDF p.7

Results

The added selection step, combined with analysis optimization, cut result loading time from 120 seconds to 10 seconds — a 92% reduction.

Loading-time callout (120s → 10s, -92%) paired with the selection-step screens — source PDF p.7
